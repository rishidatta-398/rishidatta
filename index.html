My DECA chapter elected me Director of Finance, which means the chapter's money and the paperwork behind every purchase run through me. Separately I'm one of five students who founded our Ethics Bowl club, where I keep the books and lead fundraising for a group that has grown to about fifteen members.

The two roles are usefully opposite. In DECA the money already exists and my job is to steward it carefully. In a brand new club there is nothing to administer until somebody goes and raises it.

Alongside both I'm writing a research paper on credit risk and how artificial intelligence is changing the way it gets assessed, mentored by Lavanya Prahlad at Research Spark Hub. It is the first formal research I've done and it asks considerably more of me than a classroom assignment does.

Research · Research Spark Hub

Credit risk in the age of AI

Credit risk has always been a judgment problem dressed up as a maths problem. Machine learning shifts the balance between the two: a model reads more signal than any underwriter, and inherits whatever is buried in the training data. I'm working through what that means for the people who sit outside its assumptions.
