I was born in Kettering, Ohio, and have lived in California nearly all my life. My parents emigrated from India, and my older sister, who is at UC Berkeley, has been as much a mentor to me as anyone. Between the three of them I picked up a work ethic before I picked up any particular ambition, which turned out to be the right order.

Growing up here has meant growing up around people whose backgrounds look nothing like mine, and I have come to think of that as the ordinary condition of any room worth being in.

I want to study business with an emphasis on finance or economics. I say that with the caveat that I have been learning about this field for about two years, and I am aware that is not long enough to be certain of anything. What I am certain of is the way I like to work: take the problem apart, build the model, defend the conclusion, and change my mind when someone shows me a better argument.

Outside of that, I want to travel and to be good at the non-professional parts of a life. My grandparents' generation moved across the world so that I could have the option of caring about balance, and it would be strange not to use it.

How I work

Direct, and working on it

My DECA communication assessment puts me squarely as a Driver, and the personality inventory we took calls me a Commander. Both mean roughly the same thing: I am organized, I orient hard on the end goal, and I make decisions quickly.

Those traits have an obvious downside and I would rather name it than pretend otherwise. Directness reads as impatience. A focus on the outcome makes it easy to skim the details. Being confident in a conversation is not the same as being right, and I have had to learn to ask the clarifying question instead of assuming I already understood.

So the things I'm deliberately practicing are these: listen for the detail rather than the headline, watch my tone, and do the unglamorous task properly even when I'm not the one leading. Ethics Bowl helped with the first one more than anything else, because the format only rewards you if you have genuinely heard the other team.
